Scope: full internal-consistency audit (code, data, docs, specs, CI), a user-value
assessment, and a comparative study of [algebrica.org](https://algebrica.org/) for
transferable ideas.

eslint to 10), regenerate the lockfile, drop `--legacy-peer-deps` from CI.

## 3. Making Atlas more valuable to users

Ordered roughly by leverage:

1. **Make search global.** Search is the single most common reference-site action,
   and it's buried on `/explore`. Add a `/`-key omnibox in `SiteNav` that searches
   elements, properties, discoverers, anomalies, and eras (the entity index already
   exists), and restore name/symbol filtering on the home table per the spec.
2. **Add a designed 404 + legacy redirects.** The old `/element/:symbol` and
   `/atlas/*` URL scheme is in the wild (README, any external links). Add client
   redirects for the old scheme and a catch-all NotFound page that suggests the
   nearest element/entity.
3. **Concept pages, not just entity pages.** Atlas explains *what* (values, ranks)
   but rarely *why*. First-class pages for phenomena — periodic trends, shielding
   and effective nuclear charge, lanthanide contraction, why anomalies break the
   Aufbau order — linked at first mention from folios, would turn a lookup table
   into a knowledge base. The anomaly pages are the seed of this; give trends the
   same treatment.
4. **A worked interpretation per folio.** A short "reading this element" note —
   why its ionization energy sits where it does, what its rank outliers mean —
   adds the pedagogy layer without violating the "no original chemistry prose"
   rule if sourced/derived carefully (computed comparisons are data, not prose).
5. **Provenance and freshness surface.** `SourceStrip` already distinguishes
   data/text/media sources. Add "last derived" build metadata and per-property
   source attribution so users can trust a number at a glance.
6. **Recently-viewed trail.** Reference browsing is bouncy — a small timestamped
   "your trail" list (localStorage) in the nav helps compare workflows and
   re-finding.
7. **Print/offline support.** The whole aesthetic is print-inspired; a print
   stylesheet for folios (and OG images per element for sharing) would let the
   design pay off where it naturally belongs.
8. **Finish the property system.** Wire `halfLife` in; add density/abundance if
   the seed has them; surface units and measurement conditions consistently.

## 4. Lessons from algebrica.org

Algebrica is a free, ad-free, solo-authored university-math knowledge base
(~230 entries, CC BY-NC, full Markdown+SVG source on GitHub) with a
Tufte-adjacent monochrome aesthetic — philosophically a sibling of Atlas. What
transfers:

1. **Navigation lives in the prose.** Algebrica links every concept at first
   mention; the web is the navigation. Atlas's folios should inline-link
   properties, categories, and anomalies inside the summary text, not only in
   marginalia and chips.
2. **Prerequisites as first-class metadata.** Each Algebrica entry declares the
   concepts you need first. Atlas equivalent: property pages declare "to read this
   chart: shielding, electron configuration" with links — a lightweight dependency
   graph.
3. **A site-wide ontology graph page.** Algebrica's `/graph/` presents the corpus
   as nodes and relations. Atlas's `/about/entity-map` is the seed of this —
   promote it from "about" furniture to a primary navigation surface.
4. **Content-as-data, openly published.** Algebrica ships editable Markdown +
   SVG sources and plans a SKOS/JSON semantic layer. Atlas already has the
   generated-JSON pipeline; publishing the derived dataset as a documented,
   stable artifact (with schema) would invite reuse and contributors.
5. **Transparency widgets build trust.** Algebrica has a public editorial-process
   page, per-page quality indicators, and a contribution heatmap. Atlas
   equivalents: a "how the data is derived" page (docs/data-pipeline.md exists —
   surface it in-app) and per-value provenance.
6. **Strict, tiny diagram palette.** Algebrica's figures use a 4-step grayscale
   ramp, making hundreds of diagrams coherent and printable. Atlas's 4-colour
   discipline is the same instinct — hold the line, and consider a
   grayscale-with-one-accent mode for dense visualizations.
7. **An uncluttered front door.** Algebrica opens with an epigraph, a mission
   sentence, and the index. Atlas's table-first home is already right; resist
   adding dashboard furniture.
8. **"Your history" sidebar** — directly transferable (see §3.6).
